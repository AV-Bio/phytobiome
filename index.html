Microbiome & Pathogen Library — dial each organism independently

Every organism below is a real, named species with its own persistent slider in its own field unit. They stack: run AMF + a pathogen + a virus at once and see the combined outcome.

Units, thresholds, and organism biology are drawn from the published sources cited inline in "Field notes" above (EC salinity classes, AOT40 ozone exposure, gridline-intersect AMF colonization, soil nitrate-test interpretation, germination physiology, and specific pathogen/parasite mechanisms). This is a simplified teaching model, not a calibrated instrument or a substitute for a soil/tissue lab test.

Data-source disclaimer: the alfalfa/Medicago sativa biology used directly — the Sinorhizobium meliloti symbiosis, Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. medicaginis wilt, Alfalfa mosaic virus, cold-germination response, and the entire growth-stage/root-reserve/cutting model (Kalu & Fick staging; Dhont et al. TNC data; UC Davis harvest guidance) — comes from alfalfa-specific studies. Several other organisms in the Microbiome & Pathogen Library (Rhizophagus irregularis, Trichoderma harzianum, Bacillus subtilis, Pseudomonas fluorescens/syringae, Azospirillum brasilense, Rhizoctonia solani, Ralstonia solanacearum, Meloidogyne incognita, Cuscuta campestris) are broad-host-range species whose population/severity baselines are drawn from general or other-crop research, not alfalfa-specific field data, and are applied here as reasonable proxies because that specific alfalfa data isn't published. Treat those organisms' numeric thresholds as illustrative rather than measured for this species.
Units and thresholds are drawn from the published sources cited inline in "Field notes" above. In this generalized mode, no organism is claimed as a specific species — every microbiome slider represents an unspecified member of its functional role (AMF, rhizobial symbiont, PGPR, pathogen, parasite, or virus), and the growth-stage sequence models the general vegetative→bud→flower→seedpod pattern typical of nodulating legumes rather than alfalfa's specific Kalu & Fick scale. This is a simplified teaching model, not a calibrated instrument.
